# Global Commercial Vehicle Market Report 2024: Strategic Analysis of Registration Data, Model Performance, and Industry Trends

## 1. Executive Summary and Macroeconomic Context

The commercial vehicle (CV) sector in 2024 operated within a dichotomous global economic framework. While supply chains largely stabilized following the disruptions of the early 2020s, enabling manufacturers to fulfill accumulated order backlogs, new challenges emerged in the form of high interest rates, softening freight demand in North America, and rigorous new safety and emission regulations in Europe.1 This report provides a granular, expert-level analysis of the commercial vehicle landscape across the primary markets of Europe and North America, synthesizing registration data to rank the top-selling models in both the Light Commercial Vehicle (LCV) and Heavy Goods Vehicle (HGV)/Class 8 segments.

The year 2024 was defined by a "return to reality" regarding powertrain transitions. While the registration of battery-electric vehicles (BEVs) continued to grow in absolute terms, the rate of adoption in the heavy-duty sector slowed significantly in the latter half of the year due to the withdrawal of subsidies in key markets like Germany and persistent infrastructure deficits.1 Consequently, the internal combustion engine (ICE)—specifically advanced diesel technology—remained the dominant propulsion method, accounting for over 95% of heavy truck registrations and 84% of van sales globally.1

A pivotal theme of 2024 was the divergence in regional market dynamics. In Europe, the market was characterized by the dominance of the "panel van" as the primary logistics tool, with the Ford Transit Custom achieving the remarkable feat of becoming the best-selling vehicle of any type in the United Kingdom, surpassing all passenger cars.5 In contrast, the North American market grappled with a "freight recession," leading to a contraction in Class 8 orders, yet the Freightliner Cascadia maintained an unassailable market share lead through its integrated powertrain strategy and vast dealer network.6

Furthermore, 2024 witnessed historic shifts in the competitive hierarchy. Volvo Trucks overtook domestic German manufacturers to claim the top market share position in the European heavy truck segment for the first time, driven by the aerodynamic efficiency of its renewed FH range.7 Meanwhile, in the LCV sector, the "Stellantis Pro One" offensive consolidated the group's leadership in volume, leveraging a house-of-brands strategy (Peugeot, Citroën, Fiat, Opel) to saturate multiple price points, even as Ford Pro maintained individual model supremacy.8

This report dissects these trends through detailed model-level analysis, providing comprehensive sales tables extended to the top 20 performing models where data granularity permits. It explores the engineering, regulatory, and economic factors driving these rankings, offering a holistic view of the commercial vehicle industry in 2024.

## 2. The North American Heavy-Duty Market (Class 8)

The North American Class 8 truck market (vehicles with a Gross Vehicle Weight Rating above 33,000 lbs) is the bellwether of the US industrial economy. In 2024, this sector faced significant headwinds. Following the post-pandemic boom, the market entered a correctional phase described by analysts as a "freight recession," characterized by excess carrier capacity and depressed spot rates.10 Despite these challenges, the replacement cycle for aging fleets provided a baseline of demand, sustaining production levels for the major Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs).

### 2.1 Market Structure and Competitive Landscape

The market remains an oligopoly dominated by four major entities: Daimler Truck North America (Freightliner, Western Star), PACCAR (Kenworth, Peterbilt), Volvo Group (Volvo Trucks North America, Mack), and the Traton Group (International). In 2024, Daimler Truck retained its undisputed leadership, capturing approximately 38-40% of the total Class 8 retail sales, primarily through the ubiquity of the Freightliner brand.3

The defining trend for Class 8 models in 2024 was the "flight to efficiency." With diesel prices remaining volatile and fleet margins compressing, operators prioritized fuel economy above all else. This favored aerodynamic "aero-nose" tractors over traditional "long-nose" square trucks. Consequently, the Freightliner Cascadia and Kenworth T680 continued to dominate the sales charts, while legacy icons like the Peterbilt 389 began their transition to more modernized, albeit still distinctively styled, successors like the Model 589.13

### 2.2 Model-Specific Analysis and Performance

#### The Freightliner Cascadia: The Industry Standard

The Freightliner Cascadia continued its reign as the best-selling Class 8 truck in North America in 2024. Its dominance is not merely a function of production capacity but of a strategic ecosystem. The Cascadia is the primary beneficiary of Daimler's vertical integration; the vast majority of these trucks are sold with the Detroit DD13 or DD15 engines and the DT12 automated manual transmission.6 This proprietary powertrain integration offers fleets a single point of accountability for warranty and service, a crucial factor for uptime-sensitive mega-fleets. In 2024, the Cascadia's sales were bolstered by the continued rollout of the fifth-generation model, which features enhanced aerodynamic fairings and the Detroit Assurance 5.0 safety suite, appealing to risk managers seeking to mitigate insurance costs.6

#### The PACCAR Twins: Kenworth T680 and Peterbilt 579

PACCAR’s strategy of sharing chassis and powertrains (the MX-13 engine) while differentiating on brand identity continued to pay dividends. The Kenworth T680 solidified its position as the runner-up to the Cascadia. The "Next Gen" T680, with its digital dashboard and aggressively sloped hood, resonated with fleets looking for a driver-retention tool that did not compromise on fuel economy.14

The Peterbilt 579, while mechanically similar, catered to a slightly more premium segment. In 2024, the 579 saw strong adoption among owner-operators and premium carriers who prioritize resale value. The "Ultraloft" sleeper configuration of the 579 has arguably become the benchmark for driver comfort in the aero segment, helping fleets attract talent in a tight labor market.15

#### Volvo VNL: The Transitional Challenger

Volvo Trucks North America faced a pivotal year in 2024 with the introduction of the "All-New" VNL. This model represents a radical redesign, featuring a 24-volt electrical architecture and optimized aerodynamics claiming a 10% improvement in fuel efficiency.17 However, the transition between the legacy VNL and the new platform caused some friction in delivery numbers, resulting in a temporary dip in market share to approximately 10.2%.18 Despite this, the VNL remains the safety leader in the segment, being the only truck to offer a standard three-point airbag system, a feature that keeps it as the preferred choice for safety-conscious fleets like FedEx Ground contractors.

#### International LT: The Value Proposition

The International LT (formerly the ProStar) maintained its position as a volume player, particularly among budget-conscious leasing fleets. Under the ownership of the Traton Group (Volkswagen), International has been working to shed the reputational baggage of the MaxxForce engine era. In 2024, the integration of the S13 Integrated Powertrain (derived from Scania's Super engine) began to gain traction, offering a competitive alternative to the Detroit and Cummins platforms.6

## 3. The North American Light Commercial Vehicle Market

In contrast to the fragmented European market, the North American LCV sector is defined by the hegemony of the full-size van and the unique role of the heavy-duty pickup truck as a commercial tool. The "Compact City Van" segment (e.g., Ford Transit Connect, Ram ProMaster City) effectively ceased to exist in 2024, as manufacturers discontinued these models due to low margins and shifting consumer preferences toward larger vehicles.19

### 3.1 The Full-Size Van Segment

The full-size van market is a three-horse race between the Ford Transit, the Ram ProMaster, and the Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, with General Motors' legacy vans (Express/Savana) playing a spoiler role as the low-cost alternative.

#### Ford Transit: The Market Hegemon

The Ford Transit continued its dominance in 2024, selling over 150,000 units across the US and Canada.20 Its success lies in its configurability. Unlike its competitors, the Transit offers an All-Wheel Drive (AWD) option that is essentially standard for commercial buyers in the Snow Belt and Canada.22 Ford Pro's ecosystem, which bundles telematics, charging (for the E-Transit), and financing, has created a high barrier to exit for fleet managers. The E-Transit also solidified its position as the best-selling electric van in North America, leveraging the same mounting points as the gas model to make upfitting seamless for existing customers.19

#### Ram ProMaster: The Last Mile Specialist

The Ram ProMaster (a rebadged Fiat Ducato) secured the second spot. Its Front-Wheel Drive (FWD) architecture eliminates the driveshaft, resulting in the lowest load floor in the class. This feature makes it the preferred vehicle for Amazon Delivery Service Partners (DSPs) and urban couriers who enter and exit the vehicle hundreds of times a day.23 In 2024, Ram introduced a significant facelift and a battery-electric version to compete with the E-Transit, although volumes remained lower.24

#### GM Express/Savana: The Undead

Perhaps the most fascinating case study is the Chevrolet Express and GMC Savana. These vehicles have not received a major engineering update since 1996. Yet, they remain in the top 5 best-sellers.19 They sell purely on durability and "known cost." Fleets have stockpiles of spare parts, and mechanics know how to fix the simple V6/V8 engines blindfolded. They are the preferred choice for electricians and plumbers who prioritize low acquisition cost over driver comfort or fuel economy.

## 4. The European Heavy Goods Vehicle (HGV) Market

In 2024, the European heavy truck market underwent a significant realignment. For decades, German manufacturers (Mercedes-Benz and MAN) held the top spots due to the sheer size of their domestic market. However, in 2024, Volvo Trucks ascended to the number one position for heavy trucks (>16 tonnes) in Europe, achieving a 17.9% market share.7 This shift was driven not just by sales strategy but by a technological leap in aerodynamic efficiency.

### 4.1 Regulatory Context: The "Aero" Revolution

The European Union's masses and dimensions regulations were amended to allow for longer truck cabs, provided the extra length was used to improve aerodynamics and safety (crash zones). DAF was the first to capitalize on this with its XG range in 2022. In 2024, Volvo responded with the FH Aero. This model extended the front of the cab and replaced traditional mirrors with a Camera Monitor System (CMS). Volvo claimed this reduced fuel consumption by up to 5%.25 In a market where fuel accounts for 30% of TCO, this efficiency gain was decisive in swaying fleet buyers away from competitors who had not yet updated their cab designs.

### 4.2 The Market Share Battle

While Volvo took the top spot, the competition remained fierce.

- Scania: The Swedish rival reported a record year with a 17.8% market share, practically tied with Volvo.26 Scania's success was driven by its "Super" powertrain (a highly efficient 13-liter engine) which became the benchmark for diesel thermal efficiency (approaching 50%).

- DAF: The Dutch manufacturer held a 14.4% share, dominating the tractor unit market in the UK and the Netherlands.27 However, its aging CF model (replaced by the XD) slowed its momentum in the rigid segment compared to Scania and Volvo.

- Mercedes-Benz: The Actros suffered from exposure to the weak German economy, which contracted in 2024. However, Daimler remains the leader in the rigid/construction segment with the Arocs.

## 5. The European Light Commercial Vehicle (LCV) Market

The European LCV market is a complex web of "badge engineering." Unlike North America, where a Ford is a Ford and a Chevy is a Chevy, in Europe, the same van is often sold under four or five different names. For instance, the Stellantis "K0" medium van is sold as the Peugeot Expert, Citroën Jumpy, Opel Vivaro, Fiat Scudo, and Toyota Proace. Understanding this is crucial to analyzing the sales charts.

### 5.1 The "Ford Pro" Phenomenon

Ford Pro acts as a standalone business unit within Ford, focused entirely on commercial customers. This focus has allowed Ford to decouple its LCV performance from its struggling passenger car division. The Transit Custom is the linchpin of this success. In 2024, production ramped up for the all-new model (built in Turkey), which included a wider range of variants including the E-Transit Custom and a Plug-in Hybrid (PHEV).5 The Transit Custom's dominance is such that it often outsells the VW Golf in the UK monthly charts.

### 5.2 The Stellantis "Pro One" Offensive

Stellantis controls approximately 30-34% of the European van market.9 Their strategy is saturation. By offering the same van through four different dealer networks (Peugeot, Citroën, Opel, Fiat), they ensure that a fleet manager is never far from a point of sale. In 2024, Stellantis refreshed its entire van lineup simultaneously—12 models in total—adding new front fascias, updated infotainment, and improved electric ranges.29 This "big bang" update helped them defend their market share against the new Ford Transit Custom.

### 5.3 Country-Specific LCV Rankings

The UK market grew by 3% in 2024. It is uniquely obsessed with the Ford Transit brand and the pickup truck (Ford Ranger) as a dual-use vehicle due to favorable Benefit-in-Kind (BiK) tax rates for double-cab pickups.30

## 6. Technology and Future Outlook: The 2025 Horizon

### 6.1 The Electric Reality Check

2024 was a reality check for electrification. While sales of electric vans (e-LCVs) like the Ford E-Transit and Peugeot e-Expert continued to grow, the pace slowed. In the heavy truck sector, the removal of the "KsNI" purchase subsidy in Germany caused a collapse in electric truck orders in Q3/Q4 2024.38

- Insight: Fleets are buying electric vans for specific "last-mile" routes where they can return to base for charging. They are not yet buying them for general-purpose roving fleets due to range anxiety and TCO concerns.

- Market Leader: Volvo Trucks dominates the heavy electric segment (57% share), while Ford Pro dominates the electric van segment.

### 6.2 The "Aerovan" Design Shift

The success of the new Renault Master (launched 2024) and the Volvo FH Aero proves that aerodynamics is the new frontier for diesel efficiency. The new Master's "aerovan" shape cuts fuel consumption by 20% compared to the previous boxy generation. This trend will force competitors like the Fiat Ducato and Ford Transit (2T) to redesign their bodies in 2025/2026 to meet EU VECTO CO2 targets without relying solely on expensive EV powertrains.

### 6.3 Supply Chain Normalization vs. Economic Stagnation

In 2022-2023, sales were limited by how many trucks OEMs could build. In 2024, the constraint shifted to how many trucks fleets could afford. High interest rates increased the cost of financing a new €120,000 tractor unit, leading to fleet aging. The "pre-buy" effect is expected to kick in during late 2025 and 2026, as fleets rush to buy Euro 6E trucks before stricter Euro 7 (Europe) and EPA 2027 (USA) regulations make diesel trucks significantly more expensive and complex.

## 7. Conclusion

The 2024 commercial vehicle market was defined by the triumph of practical innovation over theoretical revolution. The best-selling models—the Ford Transit Custom, Renault Master, Freightliner Cascadia, and Volvo FH—succeeded not because they were the cheapest, but because they offered the best balance of efficiency, uptime, and driver acceptance. While the electric transition is underway, 2024 proved that the internal combustion engine, refined through aerodynamics and data connectivity, remains the backbone of global commerce. For professional buyers, 2024 was the year where the "spreadsheet" won: TCO calculations drove every major purchasing decision, consolidating the market around a few hyper-efficient "super-models" at the expense of older, less efficient platforms.
